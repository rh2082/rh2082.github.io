title: About Me

I am a PhD student studying solar flares, at UCL/MSSL. I use the EUI (Extreme Ultraviolet Imager) and STIX (Spectrometer/Telescope for Imaging X-rays) instruments onboard Solar Orbiter, using data from the Solar Orbiter major flare campaigns (campaign catalogue coming soon!) to study sub-structure in solar flare ribbons on timescales near-relevant to impulsive energy deposition. 

In 2025, I completed an MSc in Mathematical and Theoretical Physics at the University of Sheffield, graduating with a Distinction. I started my PhD three weeks after submitting my master's dissertation, which was titled _Modelling Active Regions with Magnetic Flux Tubes and Non-Linear Force-Free Magnetic Fields._ (I would not encourage starting a PhD this soon after finishing an MSc!) Prior to my MSc, I did my undergraduate degree in BSc Physics (Hons) at the University of Bath from 2021 to 2024. I graduated with first-class honours in the summer of 2024, and my BSc project was titled _Investigating the Impact of an Intervening Absorber on the Properties Determined from GRB Afterglow._

During my final year at Bath, I was Chair of Bath Space Society. A particular highlight of this experience was the Gannon storms in May 2024, in which we had around 60 people in a field near campus watching a truly spectacular aurora display. I also volunteered with the Students' Union as a theatre and live event technician with Backstage Technical Services. My final event with this group was as the Technical Event Manager for Bath Fringe, where I co-led a crew of 19 through a week of theatre and musical shows across three different venues. While studying my MSc, I started learning BSL and hope to achieve my level 1 qualification in future. 

I have a keen interest in space weather forecasting and public engagement. I have given a number of talks on solar physics, been a project lead at MSSL's Work Experience Week, and aided in delivering training on the science of space weather to space weather forecasters at the Met office.
