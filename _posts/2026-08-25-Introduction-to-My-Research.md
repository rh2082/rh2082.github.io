I study the Sun. 

The Sun goes round. The inside goes round all together, but on the outside, the top and bottom go round faster than the middle. This makes the big lines that go round inside the Sun get wrapped together and lie on top of each other. They come together and parts of them move from the inside of the Sun to the outside. 

There is lots of stuff moving inside the Sun, which pushes the big lines around and makes them wrap up and get angry. If they get very angry, they break. Then they fix themselves, but when they fix themselves, it lets out lots of light and stuff. We sort of understand some of what happens to let out the light and stuff after the big lines break, but we want to know lots more. 

The Sun is made up of lots and lots of very very small things. We know that when the big lines break, the very very small things near to the break go very fast. Some of them go away from the Sun, and some of them go towards the Sun. We do not know how the small things are made to go so fast, and we want to find out. When the small fast things go into the Sun, lots of light comes out. We also do not know how that light is made to come out. We can take pictures of the light to learn.

We have computers in space which can take pictures of the Sun to help us learn more about what happens when the big lines break and how the small things are made to go fast. Our eyes can see some parts of light, but there are many parts we can't see. The space computers take pictures of the parts of light we can't see. I use the pictures of light that is a bit smaller and much, much smaller than we can see. Most space computers that take pictures of light that is a bit smaller than we can see take pictures slowly which means too much light gets in. We can't see everything that happens because there is too much light and the pictures are taken too slowly. Over the last few years, we have started taking new pictures. We take these pictures much faster so we don't let in too much light. This means we can see more of what happens after the big lines break.

I am interested in understanding how the small things make the light come out. The pictures of light that is much smaller than our eyes can see tells us about different small things than the pictures of light that is only a bit smaller than our eyes can see. When we have pictures of both parts of light taken quickly, we learn what the small fast things do to the part of the Sun that they hit.
